How we approach it

The export is a contract

Every game rig has two audiences: the animators in Maya and the engine at runtime. We treat the export skeleton as a contract with its own acceptance test — count, orientation, bind pose, naming, morph target names — so nothing surprises you during integration.

Budgets up front, not discovered late

Joint counts are agreed and split by region before a joint is placed: how many for the face, the hands, the cloth, the extras. That keeps the rig cheap without losing the deformation where the camera actually looks.

FAQ

Questions studios ask

Do you rig for Unity as well as Unreal Engine?

Yes. The export skeleton is built for your target engine, and the checks we run before delivery follow that engine’s requirements.

Can you keep a character under a joint budget?

Yes. The budget is agreed and split by region before rigging starts, and every delivery is checked against it.

Can you work with our existing game skeleton?

Yes. We can rig to a skeleton you already ship with, so animations stay compatible across characters.
