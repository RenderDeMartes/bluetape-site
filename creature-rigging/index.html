How we approach it

An animal is not a rotated human

A dog’s hind leg bends the other way from a person’s knee because the joint that looks like a knee is an ankle. Rigging it as a rotated human leg makes every walk cycle a fight. We rig from the real anatomy, so the controls match how the animal actually moves.

Wings animators can pose

A wing with dozens of feathers needs a structure that poses as one shape, then lets an animator break individual feathers where a shot calls for it. We build the fan as a driven system with manual offsets on top.

Chains that hit the frame

Tails and necks get both an authored FK layer and a smooth spline layer, blendable. Simulation can sit on top, but never instead of animation control, because a creature still has to hit a specific silhouette on a specific frame.

FAQ

Questions studios ask

Do you rig quadrupeds for games as well as film?

Yes. For games we agree the joint budget per region up front and validate the export skeleton; for animation and VFX the budget is set by deformation quality instead.

Can you rig wings and birds?

Yes. Wing systems with a posable primary chain, feather groups that follow it and per-feather offsets are a core part of our creature work.

Can you add simulation to a creature rig?

Yes, where your pipeline supports it. Simulation is layered on top of the animation controls, never a replacement for them.
