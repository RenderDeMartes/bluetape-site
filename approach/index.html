Four ways in

Pick what fits the show

01

Your system

Already on mGear, an in-house autorig or another framework? We work inside it, follow its conventions, and the rigs look like your rigs.

02

Built by hand

Some characters are better rigged from scratch. Hero faces, odd anatomy, one-off props: bespoke setups when a template would cost more than it saves.

03

A new system

Need an autorig or rigging framework of your own? We design and build one around your shows, engine and naming, and hand it over documented.

04

Our framework

When the choice is ours, we often reach for Mutant Tools, our own modular framework for Maya. Fast for creatures and game rigs — and optional.

Whatever the system

What stays the same

01

Your conventions

Naming, folder structure, versioning and publishing follow your studio. Send a document or an example rig and the deliveries match it.

02

Layered chains

Tails, necks and spines get an authored FK layer and a smooth spline layer, blendable. Simulation on top where the pipeline supports it — never instead of, because animation has to hit a specific silhouette on a specific frame.

03

Engine ready

Joint budgets are agreed and split by region up front. The export skeleton is treated as a contract with its own acceptance test: count, orientation, bind pose, naming, morph target names.

04

Validated delivery

Every asset goes through the same checks before it reaches you, whichever system built it. Failures are loud and specific, not silent.

Our own framework

Mutant Tools, when it helps

When a studio leaves the choice to us, we often build with Mutant Tools, our modular rigging framework for Maya. It is free and open source, so a rig built with it never depends on us or on a licence server.
