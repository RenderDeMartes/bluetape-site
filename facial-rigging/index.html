How we approach it

Controls first

A facial rig lives or dies by how quickly an animator can hit an expression. We agree the control layout with your team before building, and keep it consistent across every character on the show.

Joints, shapes or both

Film faces often lean on blendshapes and correctives; game faces usually have to work in joints and a small number of morph targets. We pick the approach from the target, not habit, and can match the system your studio already uses.

Held up at the extremes

Sticky lips, eyelids closing over the eye, cheeks that raise under a smile. We test the faces in the expressions the show needs, not just the neutral pose.

A good fit when
  • Your characters need facial performance, not just a jaw open
  • Game faces have to fit a strict joint budget
  • You want faces that match an existing house style of controls
  • An existing face rig doesn’t deform well close to camera
FAQ

Questions studios ask

Do you build blendshape or joint-based facial rigs?

Both, and hybrids. The choice depends on your target — film, series or game engine — and on what your pipeline already supports.

Can you match our existing facial control layout?

Yes. Matching a studio’s established layout is often the fastest route for animators.

Do you do facial rigs for games?

Yes, built inside an agreed joint budget with morph targets named for your engine.
